The controller issues the gripper-release command itself, but deliberately does not use that
command to clear its payload model. We report every eventless flight in the final campaign that
met the pre-registered eligibility criteria---successful MHE payload evidence and survival to
the release command---and report the excluded attempts separately.

\paragraph{Paired command-triggered baseline}
We retain the pre-registered paired design rather than selecting arm~B alone after the
comparison. Arm~A is a command-triggered baseline on the legacy interface: it treats the release
command as the detachment event and immediately resets payload geometry and inner-loop scaling.
Arm~B is the proposed interface. The arms also differ before release: arm~A uses the legacy,
non-continuous geometry path and schedules the inner loop by rewriting PX4 rate gains, whereas
arm~B uses the continuous $m/s/J$ frame and body-rate-command scaling. A pair enters the
comparison only when both arms are eligible. Across 23 such pairs (8/7/8 across the three
conditions) both arms complete 23/23 releases with no observed post-release instability
(Table~\ref{tab:paired-event}); arm~A has zero confirmation delay by definition.

Under delayed detachment a command-triggered baseline is expected to clear prematurely; the
experiment measures this temporal mismatch and the confirmation delay that evidence-based updates
incur. A fault injector received the normal release command but retained the physical
DetachableJoint for a median \SI{4.01}{s}. Of twelve arm-runs, one arm~A run never armed PX4,
leaving five valid pairs and one valid unpaired arm~B run. In all five pairs arm~A cleared its
payload model at the command, a median \SI{4.01}{s} before physical detachment. Arm~B cleared
before detachment in none of its six valid runs: it entered \textsc{unresolved} in 5/6 and then
confirmed a median \SI{12.45}{s} after detachment, and the remaining run confirmed \SI{3.00}{s}
after detachment. All eleven valid runs had an independent Gazebo \texttt{DETACHED} record.
Median position-error peaks during the hold were \SI{0.071}{m} (A) and \SI{0.068}{m} (B), so the
result supports a difference in model--plant consistency, not a tracking advantage or crash
avoidance. We did not test a permanently jammed gripper, under which arm~A would carry a loaded
vehicle with an empty model.

\paragraph{Completion and delay of the complete interface}
The complete interface confirmed release in 31/31 eligible flights with no observed
post-release instability (Table~\ref{tab:complete}; two-sided \SI{95}{\percent}
Clopper--Pearson interval $[\SI{88.8}{\percent},\SI{100}{\percent}]$). This is a conditional
closed-loop result for the interface as a whole, not a validation of any single release
detector: Table~\ref{tab:paths} shows that three confirmation paths occurred.

The confirmation delay is \emph{bimodal}. In \SI{42}{\percent} of flights the confidence
crosses threshold while the vehicle is still flying the figure-eight, and confirmation completes
in a median \SI{3.46}{s} (Fig.~\ref{fig:mission}). In the remaining \SI{58}{\percent} no release
evidence is available in maneuver---$\hat s$ has drifted under sustained excitation or the frozen
reference never formed---so the system reaches its \SI{12}{s} timeout, declares
\textsc{unresolved}, and executes a \SI{3}{s} smooth brake to hover; confirmation then completes
at a median \SI{16.48}{s}. Table~\ref{tab:complete} keeps the two modes separate.

No flight in any path declared release before the command. In 26 of the 31 flights the
payload-presence machine made exactly two transitions, one at grasp and one at release; in the
five confidence-only flights it never declared \textsc{empty} within the logged window (over
\SI{40}{s} past confirmation). Four of the 31 load declarations were raised by the estimator's
own directional thrust-residual evidence rather than sustained mass evidence; no external grasp
signal was consumed. The published first moment decayed continuously rather than stepping to
zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:moment}).

\paragraph{Development-stage evidence}
Two earlier tests motivated the final design but used earlier code and do not validate the final
detector. A quantity--residual fast branch without moment evidence declared release prematurely
in 3/9 randomized flights during a \SI{4}{m/s} figure-eight; loaded-flight mass estimates could
remain below the nominal empty threshold for up to \SI{48}{s}. After its removal, a 12-flight
randomized test of the moment-gated detector, run before the frozen reference was introduced,
produced no pre-command declaration, 11 moment-gated releases (median detector delay
\SI{1.9}{s}), and one flight that stayed \textsc{unresolved}.

\paragraph{Attempts and exclusions}
The paired campaign comprised 81 attempts. Arm~B (proposed) made 40: 31 eligible, six crashed
before the release command, and three lacked MHE payload evidence (the logs cannot distinguish
capture failure from non-recognition). Arm~A made 41: 30 eligible and 11 pre-release crashes.
Because the arms differ before release, these rates are reported per arm rather than pooled or
compared, and neither characterizes end-to-end mission reliability. Table~\ref{tab:paired-event}
uses the 23 pairs with both arms eligible; Tables~\ref{tab:complete}--\ref{tab:paths} use all 31
eligible arm~B flights. Tail-window tracking is not compared, because arm~B brakes to hover
after an unresolved timeout while arm~A continues the figure-eight.
