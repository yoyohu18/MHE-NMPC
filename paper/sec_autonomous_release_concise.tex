The controller issues the gripper-release command itself but does not use it to clear its
payload model. We report every eventless flight in which the payload physically attached (from
the gripper's attach record) and the vehicle survived to the release command. The
pre-registered rule judged attachment from MHE mass evidence instead, which silently excluded
flights whose estimator failed to recognize an attached payload.

\paragraph{Paired command-triggered baseline}
We retain the pre-registered paired design rather than selecting arm~B alone after the
comparison. Arm~A is a command-triggered baseline on the legacy interface: it treats the release
command as the detachment event and immediately resets payload geometry and inner-loop scaling.
Arm~B is the proposed interface. The arms also differ before release: arm~A uses the legacy,
non-continuous geometry path and schedules the inner loop by rewriting PX4 rate gains, whereas
arm~B uses the continuous $m/s/J$ frame and body-rate-command scaling. A pair enters the
comparison only when both arms are eligible. Across 19 such pairs (8/3/8 across the three
conditions) both arms complete 19/19 releases with no observed post-release instability
(Table~\ref{tab:paired-event}); arm~A has zero confirmation delay by definition. Only three
\SI{0.30}{kg}/\SI{4}{m/s} pairs formed because arm~A crashed during or immediately after lift in 11 of 16 attempts
at that condition (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).

Under delayed detachment a command-triggered baseline is expected to clear prematurely; the
experiment measures this temporal mismatch and the confirmation delay that evidence-based updates
incur. A fault injector received the normal release command but retained the physical
DetachableJoint for a median \SI{4.01}{s}. Of twelve arm-runs, one arm~A run never armed PX4,
leaving five valid pairs and one valid unpaired arm~B run. In all five pairs arm~A cleared its
payload model at the command, a median \SI{4.01}{s} before physical detachment. Arm~B cleared
before detachment in none of its six valid runs: it entered \textsc{unresolved} in 5/6 and then
confirmed a median \SI{12.45}{s} after detachment, and the remaining run confirmed \SI{3.00}{s}
after detachment. All eleven valid runs had an independent Gazebo \texttt{DETACHED} record.
Median position-error peaks during the hold were \SI{0.071}{m} (A) and \SI{0.068}{m} (B), so the
result supports a difference in model--plant consistency, not a tracking advantage or crash
avoidance. We did not test a permanently jammed gripper, under which arm~A would carry a loaded
vehicle with an empty model.

\paragraph{Command-armed confirmation and unsignaled loss}
The hold result reflects persistent confirmation, not eventlessness: a variant armed only by the
command (arm~C, otherwise identical to arm~B, confirming on a \SI{3}{s} thrust drop) also never
cleared early (0/6 pairs) and confirmed sooner after detachment (median \SI{4.3}{s} versus
\SI{7.3}{s}). Eventlessness matters when no release command reaches the controller---a gripper
that opens by itself, a load torn off, or a release triggered elsewhere whose notification is
lost. With such losses injected at the release phase, arm~B detected the loss in 10 of 18 paired
flights (4/9 at \SI{2}{m/s}, 4/5 and 2/4 at \SI{0.15}{kg} and \SI{0.30}{kg}/\SI{4}{m/s}; median
latency \SIrange{2.2}{2.9}{s}), while arm~C, never armed, detected none and kept a phantom first
moment. Seven of the eight misses occurred because the loaded moment reference had not formed,
or the payload had not been recognized, before the loss. The cost, a detector exposed throughout loaded flight, was not observed: no release was
declared before a command or injected loss in 209 loaded flights (\SI{3.7}{h}; per-flight
\SI{95}{\percent} upper bound \SI{1.4}{\percent}).

\paragraph{Completion and delay of the complete interface}
The complete interface confirmed release in 32 of 36 eligible flights (Table~\ref{tab:complete};
two-sided \SI{95}{\percent} Clopper--Pearson interval
$[\SI{73.9}{\percent},\SI{96.9}{\percent}]$). All four failures, at \SI{4}{m/s}, trace to estimator failure
(Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}): three never recognized the payload and stayed \textsc{unresolved}, and
one diverged after release. The pre-registered rule would have excluded the first three
(31/32). This is a
conditional closed-loop result for the interface as a whole, not a validation of any single
release detector: Table~\ref{tab:paths} shows that three confirmation paths occurred.

The confirmation delay is \emph{bimodal}. In 17 of the 32 completed flights the confidence
crosses threshold while the vehicle is still flying the figure-eight, and confirmation completes
in a median \SI{3.46}{s} (Fig.~\ref{fig:mission}). In the remaining 15 no release
evidence is available in maneuver---$\hat s$ has drifted under sustained excitation or the frozen
reference never formed---so the system reaches its \SI{12}{s} timeout, declares
\textsc{unresolved}, and executes a \SI{3}{s} smooth brake to hover; confirmation then completes
at a median \SI{16.56}{s}. Table~\ref{tab:complete} keeps the two modes separate.

No flight in any path declared release before the command. In 23 of the 32 completed flights
the payload-presence machine made exactly two transitions, one at grasp and one at release; in
the nine confidence-only flights it never declared \textsc{empty} within the logged window (over
\SI{40}{s} past confirmation). Every load declaration came from sustained mass evidence; no
external grasp signal was consumed. The published first moment decayed continuously rather than stepping to
zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:moment}).

\paragraph{Partial payload loss}
Four \SI{0.05}{kg} boxes were grasped together and three were detached one at a time (45, 80
and \SI{115}{s} after grasp) with no signal and no release command; a control arm carried the
same boxes without loss (five alternating valid pairs per speed). Pre-registered criteria: (a)
$\hat m$ enters $\pm\SI{0.02}{kg}$ of each new truth in $\ge\SI{80}{\percent}$ of steps; (b)
paired tracking-RMS difference $\le\SI{0.02}{m}$, no crash; (c) no \textsc{empty} declaration
after the first two losses. At \SI{2}{m/s} all criteria pass: $\hat m$ followed 14 of 15 steps (median convergence
\SIrange{1.1}{8.8}{s}) with no false \textsc{empty} and tracking indistinguishable from the
control arm. At \SI{4}{m/s} (a) reached only its threshold (12/15), so we claim recovery at
\SI{2}{m/s} only.

\paragraph{Development-stage evidence}
Two earlier tests motivated the final design but used earlier code and do not validate the final
detector. A quantity--residual fast branch without moment evidence declared release prematurely
in 3/9 randomized flights during a \SI{4}{m/s} figure-eight. After its removal, a 12-flight
randomized test of the moment-gated detector, run before the frozen reference was introduced,
produced no pre-command declaration, 11 moment-gated releases (median detector delay
\SI{1.9}{s}), and one flight that stayed \textsc{unresolved}.

\paragraph{Attempts and exclusions}
The paired campaign comprised 84 attempts. Arm~B (proposed) made 42: 36 eligible, five crashed
before the release command (three in the \SI{0.30}{kg}/\SI{4}{m/s} figure-eight, two during
lift at \SI{2}{m/s}), and one did not physically attach. Arm~A made 42: 25 eligible and 17
pre-release crashes, 11 of them during or just after lift at \SI{0.30}{kg}/\SI{4}{m/s}. Because the arms
differ before release, these rates are reported per arm rather than pooled or compared, and
neither characterizes end-to-end mission reliability. Table~\ref{tab:paired-event} uses the 19
pairs with both arms eligible; Table~\ref{tab:complete} uses all 36 eligible arm~B flights and
Table~\ref{tab:paths} the 32 that completed. Tail-window tracking is not compared, because arm~B brakes to hover
after an unresolved timeout while arm~A continues the figure-eight.
